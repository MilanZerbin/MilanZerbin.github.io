## Seminar: AI in Math

In the summer term 24 [Kai Cieliebak](https://www.uni-augsburg.de/en/fakultaet/mntf/math/prof/geom/kai-cieliebak/) and I hosted a joint Bachelor/Master seminar on the hot topic AI in Math.

For more detail, see [the course announcement](/teaching/kisem25/announcement.pdf) (in german).

## Material

The seminar was separated into two blocks:

 1. Talks on different usages of AI in Math given by Master Students, attended by everyone:
    - Two talks discussing [this paper using machine learnig for knot invariants](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.15323)
    - Two talks discussing [this paper using machine learning for arithmetic curves](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.04084)
    - a general talk introducing the basic workings of llms [as presented here](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.04556)
 2. A weekend-blockseminar for the bachelor students and especially eager master students:
    - Using different LLMs and prompting techniques to try to solve many bachelor level exercises to perfection.
    - document and learn from findings and risks
    - discuss the results in the group


### Results

The results from the weekend were surprising to some colleagues so we list them here:

 - Of the Shelf LLMs at the time solve around 30% of the posed exercises perfectly on first try. 37% more are almost perfectly solved.
 - In ~40% of the cases, a second try at the LLM finds all or most mistakes present in the solution.
 - In 20% of the cases, nonexisting errors were hallucinated upon asking.
 - A slighly more powerful agentic system our student Jörn Stöhler built was already able to perfectly solve almost all posed bachelor level exercises.

For more details, see the [presentation we held for the didactics chair](/activities/kidida).
