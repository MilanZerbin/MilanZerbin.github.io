## Method

![The three pillars of a successful lecture (in german)](/teaching/ana26/schaubild.png)

As is usual, the concept rests on the three pillars

 1. Lecture: In Presence, One big group
 2. Tutorial Sessions: In Presence, small groups
 3. Exercise Sheets: Mostly remote, smaller groups/individually

Supporting the middle pillar, the excercises, there is some additional methods employed.

 4. Global tutoring session: Peer-Grading Excercise sheets.
 5. Grading: More but smaller exams

In the end, the goal is of course that the students (and secondary, but also important, our tutors) learned something and perform well in evaluation. So far so generic.

The reason for tactical changes inside this framework are that 

 - [of the shelf LLMs can almost perfectly solve bachelor level mathematics problems](teaching/aisem.md)
    - We can not anymore give (a part of) the grade for homework!
    - Why visit a lecture/tutorial session when you have a seemingly all-knowing tutor in your pocket always?
    - How do students learn a helpful usage of LLMs in their day to day math life?
    - How do we keep the students from falling into the illusion of competence/fluency illusion?
 - [there is loads of high quality educational content already out there](https://www.3blue1brown.com/)
    - What is the selling point of in person teaching?
    - How do we incorporate great resources when they fit?
 - Didactical research suggests that some changes are beneficial anyways
    - [Short breaks can increase the reception rate of the lecture contents]()
    - [Sitting long periods of time can damage the body]()
    - [Interactive teaching can increase engagement with the contents]()

We still believe, that an enthusiastic lecturer can give an in presence lecture that energizes the audience and will be preferred to or a valuable addition to online resources. 
Hence the smallest changes are to 

 1. Lecture: 
    - mainly classical lecture.
    - two 40 minute blocks separated by a 5 minute movement break
    - every 40 minute block interrupted by a small engagement question for the audience

We wish to protect our tutors from the toxic environment that having a seemingly all knowing LLM in the room constantly can create in their sessions. 
Furtermore, one of the most important skills we wish the students to acquire is the *skill to reason and talk about mathematics with colleagues*. 
To accomplish both, we change the tutorial sessions a bit more.

 2. Tutorial Sessions:
    - There will be no technical devices, and no books/lecture notes allowed in the sessions.
    - The students will collaboratively discuss the truth values of a given set of statements about the weeks lecture contents.
    - The tutors together come up with the statements, and guide the students during the session.
    - Results are discussed in Group.


Lastly, the backbone of learning mathematics, the exercise sheets. 
These undergo the most drastic changes due to the changed circumstances. 

 3. Exercise Sheets:
    - Not mandatory or grade relevant.
    - Solutions are auto-generated by an LLM and checked by tutors, then made public.
    - Once a week all students assemble in a room and discuss the excercises. This happens in the Global Tutoring session.

 4. Global Tutoring session:
    - Students form Groups of three.
    - A few of this weeks excercises (selected by me) are communicated.
    - The students have 30-45 min to write up as as a Group of three their best attempt at a solution to the selected excercises.
    - Common misconceptions and traps are discussed in plenary.
    - The student Groups now exchange their sheets and grade their peers.

Both the tutorial sessions and the global tutoring session should help the students to realistically check their knowledge. However we believe, that having a seemingly all-knowing tutor in your pocket at all times makes it still all too easy to fall into a trap of makebelief. Hence:

 5. Grading:
    - Three equally spaced tests over the course of the semester, each focussing on the contents of the lecture that were introduced since the last test.
    - End exam about the entire contents of the course.
    - tests and exam together compute the grade (each test gives half as much credits as the end exam, with the worst test being automatically not taken into account).

We believe that more small exams are much better than only a big one at the end.
